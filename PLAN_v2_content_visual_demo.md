## 1. The design mandate: visual-first, low text

This is the most important rule in the document. A viewer will not read paragraphs. Every section leads with one idea, one short line, and a visual or interactive element that carries the meaning. Depth lives behind progressive disclosure, never in a wall of text.

**The conversion rule:** any list longer than about four items becomes a visual device, never visible bullets. The toolkit:

- **Interactive lifecycle diagram** for anything that spans pre-deal to exit.
- **Hover or click reveal chips** instead of bulleted capability lists.
- **Tabs and accordions** for "what we assess" or "what we do" depth.
- **Demo widgets** as the proof, in place of describing what the agents do.
- **Toggle** for either/or content (firm level vs portfolio level).
- **Compact icon plus one-line tiles** for problem lists and outcomes.
- **A simple architecture diagram** for the agentic layer.

Ceiling per section: if more than roughly 40 words of body copy are visible at once, convert the rest into a visual or a reveal. Headlines and one supporting line stay as text. Everything else earns its place as something you look at, not read.

Three worked examples, so the pattern is clear:

- Technology Due Diligence "what we assess" (architecture, applications, spend, cyber, technical debt, org, vendors, M&A readiness) becomes an interactive **diligence scan**: eight domains in a grid that surface a one-line finding on hover, tied to the W3 demo. Not a list.
- CIO Advisory "what we do" (eight items) becomes a compact **chip cloud** grouped in two columns, revealed on scroll. Not a list.
- The agent catalog becomes a **visual grid of agent cards**, each an icon plus one line, two of them linking to a live demo. Not a list.

- **Differentiator:** CIO-level judgment, hands-on execution, and reusable agentic systems. Operator-built, usage-aligned, reusable across the platform.
- **Audience:** private equity firms, operating partners, and portfolio-company leadership.
- **Verticals:** Engineering and Construction, Infrastructure and Industrial Services, Consulting and Professional Services.
- **Ultimate outcome:** lower cost, faster execution, lower risk, better operating performance, stronger exit readiness.

Your advantage against Infosys and Accenture is the opposite of breadth: specialization, senior technology leadership, speed, and agentic execution. AI is the differentiated execution capability, not the entire identity.

**P1, build first, these carry the site:**

- **W1 Portfolio Monitor (Hero).** Compact card: mono label, one live dot, three or four holding rows with health score and status, one rising trend line, three mini stats (Monitoring 12, Alerts 8, Updated 4s ago). Hover a row emphasizes its point on the line. On view: line draws, rows stagger in, stats count up, dot pulses once. Build: shadcn Card and Badge, hand-rolled SVG line.

Part B, four tabs each with one clean output: GOVERNANCE (standards checklist across holdings), INTELLIGENCE (KPI and flags table resolving to one insight line), DEPLOYMENT (100-day integration tracker), ATTRIBUTION (spend against EBITDA impact, two lines). Build: shadcn Tabs, Card, Table, Badge, Progress.

**P1.5, light interactive:**

- **Firm vs Portfolio toggle (Home).** Swaps a chip set between the two levels. Simple, no heavy state.

**P2, build on Agentic Systems and Industries:**

- **W3 Diligence red-flag triage.** A data-room file list, a Run review action, red, amber, green flags with one-line findings, a summary count. Re-runnable, click a flag to expand. Build: shadcn Card, Button, Badge, Collapsible, Progress.

## 8. Motion

Reuse the motion system already in the build. Apple springs, critically damped by default, a small bounce only on the slider release. GSAP ScrollTrigger for reveals and the drag slider. Keep the total budget low. One orchestrated hero reveal, disciplined scroll reveals, crisp micro-interactions, demos animating once on view. Every motion respects reduced-motion. If a motion does not aid understanding or earn a small moment of delight, cut it.
